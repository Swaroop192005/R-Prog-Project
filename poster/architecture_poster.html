NYC Yellow Taxi Trip Records · January – March 2024 · 9,201,683 cleaned trips

Laboratory Assignment 8 · R Programming · Swaroop Naik (github.com/Swaroop192005/R-Prog-Project)

Problem

Millions of taxi-trip records make conventional row-by-row R code slow and memory hungry. Goal: a scalable R workflow that finds demand patterns, high-revenue routes, fare–distance behaviour and payment preferences, and that measures which R programming approach is fastest and most memory efficient.

  • purrr / apply family – functional style
  • foreach + doParallel – parallel
  • microbenchmark, system.time, gc()

Key insights

  • Demand peaks at 18:00 and bottoms at 4–5 AM; Thursday and March are the busiest weekday and month.
  • Airport routes dominate revenue: top route JFK Airport → Outside of NYC; airport trips are ~10% of trips but ~27% of revenue.
  • Fare is almost linear in distance (r = 0.942), but fare per mile falls sharply as trips get longer.
  • Row-wise apply() was 4461× slower than vectorized code; merge() was 21× slower than a data.table join and used 11× the memory.
  • Parallel gave no gain here: 3 partitions over 2 workers allow at most 1.5×, and worker start-up plus copying 421 MB cancels it out. A light task ran at 0.01×.

Recommendation

data.table as the core engine, vectorized element-wise logic, functional iteration over a few large units (files, columns, months), and foreach/doParallel only for CPU-heavy independent work, where the benchmark shows the speedup outweighs the start-up and data-transfer overhead.
